The Car Is Where You Sleep Now
On care, carelessness, and the limit that decides which one power becomes

Imagine the car is where you sleep most nights, when you're not back at home and there aren't any couches you can surf... for now.

Not just for one night, for months. It's off and on, but you've done it for long enough to learn where the best restrooms are, places to shower and some nights, stay warm. 

When you're at the right place at the right time and make a few hundred cash.

You still have the job. You drive to it in the car you sleep in. You shower at the gym you kept the membership for, for exactly that reason. You are never late and usually low on gas.

What happened wasn't a fall. The rent went up a few hundred dollars. The cost of everything has gone up. Groceries cost about thirty percent more than they did in January 2020. Insuring the car you sleep in costs more than half again what it did. 

The raise didn't come, and your hours got cut last week. One month the math stopped mathing. Quietly, the way that math does. Without anyone deciding anything.

Then it compounds down. Quickly sometimes. 

Nobody chose this for you... you try not to let it get to you. You journal.

"Dear journal: it was another hard day. I need a new battery for my car but they're $135 and I'm $12 short. I hope the auto parts store will let me borrow their pliers."

You were priced out. Which is a different verb than *failed*. And the thing that caused it has no face. No body. Just motion.

But let's stay in the car for one more second. It is far more ordinary than you have been told.

More than half the people who slept in an American shelter last night had earned wages that same year. The most careful study of why one city carries five times the homelessness of a poorer city, one with more addiction, found the gap wasn't drugs. Not illness. Not weather. Not a soft safety net.

It was rent.

There is no villain.

A modest two-bedroom now takes about thirty-four dollars an hour to carry without going broke. That is roughly four and a half times a federal minimum wage that has not moved since 2009. There is no county in the country where a full-time minimum-wage job clears that bar.

No one in particular decided that. 

The market ran its logic and the wage froze in time. The promised housing never got built. 

The count climbed. On a single night in January 2025, about **three-quarters of a million people** had nowhere to be in the United States.

That is what carelessness looks like at the scale of a country.

I want to believe it's not all cruelty. But we know... there's some. 

There's a harm that comes from an absence, too. A system with no one at the top. Just a network of shells and an ownership tree no one can read, standing on our shoulders, sucking up the rent.

An action that answers to nothing is not evil. It is careless. And carelessness is the one luxury the unbounded can afford.

Housing is the clearest place to watch it happen.

### The limit is not the tax on care. It is the source of it.

Now watch the same forces inside a limit. A finite space.

None of us are going to Mars... most of us aren't leaving the zip code we're in now. 

Put the same rent in a place that has to look. Where the eviction can't clear until someone stands where the family will land. Same landlord. Same market. Same math. It behaves differently.

Not because you added a rule. Because you added a bound. **And a bound is the thing that converts power into care.**

I only understood this recently, after years of building the machinery that enforces exactly these limits. Systems far less humane than housing. Moving far faster.

I watched it once, in early 2021. A fraud model I had helped tune was ready to freeze a claim in the middle of a pandemic. Someone on their way to the bank, then the grocery store. Food for the week. Work history, identity, all of it good. The problem was that someone else had tried filing in the same name.

Back then it cost something to present a passable identity and a claim. Now it's almost free.

The bound I had set made the system stop and ask before the claim was marked a hard stop. The state sent it to adjudication instead. A pause.

That pause was the whole thing. The machine, made to account for the person on the other end of the claim before it moved against them.

I had always thought of the limit as the price of safety. The tax you pay to keep power from hurting people.

It is closer to the reverse.

Care is what power becomes when something holds it.

Remove the bound and you do not get freer, kinder action. You get carelessness. There is nothing left for the action to be careful of.

Nobody was harmed by malice. They were harmed by an absence.

Which leaves the harder question. The one the rest of this is really about.

What does it take to hold?

### Different, and Held

There is a word for what a place makes when it has enough weight to hold more than one thing at once. 

Most of us hear it wrong.

When we say *harmony* we picture agreement. Unison. Everyone landing on the same note.

This is a mistranslation that sends us building the wrong thing every time. The single rulebook. The one universal system. The platform that makes everyone a subject of the same terms of use.

In Spanish, it is *armonía*. The truer meaning is nearly the opposite of unison. Harmony is what arises when different things are held in the same environment, and the environment has enough gravity to let them coexist without being made the same.

Two musicians playing different music in one square. And the square does not tear.

Different, and held.

That is much harder than agreement. Agreement only asks everyone to give something up. Harmony asks for an environment strong enough that no one has to.

### A common measure is not sameness

To hold different things and still let them mean something to one another, you need a common measure.

Two rulers, made by two different hands. Neither wrong. Neither ruling the other. They still need a way to translate one into the other. Or nothing between them can be traded. Or promised. Or repaired.

But a common measure is not sameness.

What has to be shared is the form of the scale, not the authority over it. Each place keeps its own weights. Each place answers for its own limits. They agree on how to convert between them, not on who rules them all.

Impose one measure from above and you have not built a common scale. You have handed one hand authority over everyone else.

Every few weeks I vacuum all the crumbs out at the gas station, at the same machine as everyone else. 

The human name for the common measure is shared humanity.

It is what lets two strangers with no language between them stand in the same few feet and be understood without being made alike. A hand goes to a chest, and you know exactly what was meant. That is a common measure with no sameness in it at all.

### Care holds the future. Repair holds the past.

Care is what an environment does with power going forward. Repair is what it does across a rupture.

I have been learning the difference slowly, writing a set of essays about forgiveness with a friend, Denali, who is among other things a lawyer. The real kind. Systems that punish forever, and people who forgive anyway.

The question underneath that work turned out to be the same one underneath the machinery. What decides whether an environment holds, or lets go?

A system that punishes forever is a filing cabinet that never releases. A [historical society](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/historical-society-kinetic-chris-perkins-ozz4c).

The weight becomes the identity. The conviction becomes the person. The debt becomes the name.

That is a life sentence. It is also a credit score. Look closely and they come out of the same machine.

Repair is an environment sensing that the conditions have changed, and releasing anyway. Forgiveness is repair. A trust that can be rebuilt after it breaks is repair.

Denali's clients and the systems I design face one question under it all. Can this place tell the difference between what you did and who you are becoming? And does it have enough weight to hold you while you cross from the first to the second?

Care holds the future. Repair holds the past.

They are the two things an environment with enough gravity can do. A place that manages only one of them does not have enough yet.

Care is what power becomes when something holds it. 

Forward, into who someone is becoming. 

*Now you can see the full shape of the holding.*

Backward, across what they did.

It was never a tax. It was the gravity, all the way down.

### The four things

I did not reason my way to any of this. I heard it first a year before I had the vocabulary for it. From my wife. On a Saturday in November. In the cab of our truck.

We were driving to vote in the new truck I've never had to sleep in. 

I had not understood until that drive how much of our talking the old one's road noise had been quietly swallowing for years. This one was built well enough to be quiet enough to hold a conversation.

That is the entire thesis of my work, sitting in a driveway.

She said three things across three stretches of road on the way to the county admin building. Both of us facing forward. My hands on the wheel. I asked her one question. 

Her first two came quickly. The third came from somewhere further down. She took her time with it.

She was not describing a protocol or specs. She was describing how to be a person. 

- **Recognize our shared humanity.** The common measure that lets us be held together without being made the same.
- **Take responsibility for the systems we create.** A runaway is a design, and a design can be declined. Nobody upstream gets to hand the damage to whoever is standing closest to it.
- **Sometimes, to go faster, we must slow down.** The limit that makes power careful. Care instead of carelessness. That was the third one, the one that came from further down the road.

And a fourth I thought of writing [Question Zero](https://chrisperkins505.medium.com/question-zero-e659d578cf13?source=friends_link&sk=a42cb15765cdc3406420e960660afa17): **name the aim.**

> **Name the aim.** The keystone, set before anything else, because without it there is nothing for the rest to be careful of, or careful toward.

### The gap

Go back to the car. Your tax return was mailed to your sister's house. You had enough for a new battery and that long-overdue oil change.

Suppose we build it. Suppose we set the housing market, and every faster and colder machine like it, inside an environment with enough gravity to make it careful. To make it care for us. To be...

Held.

Answerable to the person it is about to move against, us. Able to forgive and to be forgiven.

Suppose we get the whole thing right.

You are still in the car.

In the version where we fix everything upstream of you, we fix it some months too late. Years, sometimes. No environment we build now reaches backward to give you back the winter you spent sleeping in your car.

Some things care arrives too late to prevent. Some people are standing exactly where the gap is widest. A hold strong enough to keep a whole country's difference from tearing is still not a reach long enough to close around every single person inside it. We'll need to carry it the rest of the way.

That is the honest edge of all of it.

The same limit that turns power into care is the limit that makes care finite. That is her second principle, coming back around: take responsibility for the systems we create.

Enough weight to hold is not the same as enough reach to save.

I don't know what else to do than to keep showing up at the gap. Sometimes I call it the ditch. 

And to build the environment anyway. Because the alternative is power with nothing holding it, carelessness at the speed of a market or a model. In machines I've started calling it [user-directed, unbounded agency](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/meter-bound-chris-perkins-rocgc). Someone points it. Nothing holds it. The key says yes, and the action is wrong for the person on the other end. That is the thing that put you in the car to begin with.

---

I wrote once about a plaza in Madrid where three strangers who shared nothing were briefly held by a place strong enough to let them stay different and still be together. That essay was the story. This one is the physics under it.

Both are trying to say the same thing. Build the environment with enough gravity, and what emerges between the different things you are holding has a name.

*Armonía.* Not agreement. Not everyone on the same note. Different, and held.

And even then, someone is still out past the edge of it, in a car with the trunk full of their belongings. It is us in the car.

We build it anyway.
