The candlemaker’s blindness is not ignorance. It is the structural consequence of manufacturing the darkness that makes your product necessary. He cannot see that the light is borrowed because he’s the one who sealed out the free version. He has never danced. He has never spent himself on a signal that costs real energy to produce. He has never opened a window. He has only ever sold the output.


In March, before the desert blooms, the colony faces a choice it doesn’t know it’s making.

The candlemaker arrives in autumn. He takes the comb. He sells the light. He does not see the architecture he’s destroying because he has never lived inside it.

The light is beautiful. The rooms are warm. The declarations are being written.

But the wax is borrowed. The dance was someone else’s. The windows are boarded. And the spring will come whether the hive survives or not.

Who opens the window?
