Day 5: The Gravity Well
Your SOC is a feature, not a bug. A very expensive feature.

What if the most expensive part of your security budget was also the least effective?

> *“The strength of a negative feedback loop is important RELATIVE TO THE IMPACT IT IS DESIGNED TO CORRECT. If the impact increases in strength, the feedbacks have to be strengthened too.”***— Donella Meadows, Leverage Points: Places to Intervene in a System**

# Leverage Point 8: Negative Feedback Loops

We are approaching the middle of the ladder. Here, things get interesting.

In systems thinking, **Negative Feedback Loops** are self-correcting mechanisms. They are goal-seeking. A thermostat is the classic example: if the room is too hot, it turns on the AC. If it’s too cold, it turns on the heat. The loop’s purpose is to bring the system back to its desired state.

These loops are the bedrock of stability. And we have built our entire security apparatus on the most expensive, least reliable one imaginable.

# The Trap

We have decided, as an industry, that the ultimate negative feedback loop is a human being.

Specifically, a highly-trained, under-caffeinated analyst in a Security Operations Center (SOC) staring at a queue full of alerts. This is our thermostat. An alert fires—the room is too hot!—and we expect a person to manually turn the knob by investigating, triaging, and responding.

This is the Gravity Well. We are pouring hundreds of billions of dollars into a strategy that requires humans to operate at machine speed. Sometimes we give the humans a robot (SOAR). It’s an economic and strategic dead end. The impact we are trying to correct, as Meadows would say, is a machine-speed attack. Our feedback loop are people. People who need to sleep, eat, and take breaks. There’s also a job shortage.

The math doesn’t math.

Alert fatigue isn’t a side effect of this system. It is the system. Burnout isn’t a risk. It is the inevitable outcome.

We are asking people to be the governors on a machine that operates thousands of times faster than human cognition and scales beyond any team of humans can operate. We are paying a fortune for a feedback loop that is, by design, too slow to matter when it matters most.

# The Shift

We need to stop trying to build better dashboards. We need to start building a better system.

The goal is not to give the human analyst a clearer signal. The goal is to get the human out of the real-time feedback loop entirely. (#controversial! Leave a comment below!)

In Digital Gravity, the negative feedback loop is not an alert. It is the physics of the environment itself. The system must feel its own state and self-correct.

Automatically. At machine speed.

This is the practical application of the Zeroth Law:

    A ≤ E

When an agent’s Autonomy (A) begins to exceed the Environment’s Stability (E), the system doesn’t send an alert to the SOC. It *acts*.

This is the automated thermodynamic loop.

When an agent generates Heat (entropy, risk), the system applies Gravity (friction, latency, constraint). The loop is closed instantly, without human intervention.

The thermostat is the substrate.

An AI trying to exfiltrate data doesn’t trigger an alert. It experiences a sudden, massive increase in latency as the system imposes gravitational drag on its connection.

A compromised account trying to access sensitive systems finds its capabilities automatically contracting, its permissions shrinking in real time.

The feedback is the physics. The response is the physics.

# The Landing

We are burning out our best people. We have turned brilliant, creative problem-solvers into slow, expensive, burnout-prone thermostats.

The purpose of human intelligence is not to be the real-time feedback loop for a machine. It is to design the machine. To set its goals. To ask the questions.

Let’s get our people out of the Gravity Well. Let’s free them to do the work that matters. Let’s build systems that correct themselves, so our people can focus on building what comes next.
