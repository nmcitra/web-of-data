Day 7: Proprioception
Your system is numb. It needs to feel pain.

We are drowning in data, but starved for information.

> *“There was this subdivision of identical houses... the electric meter in some of the houses was installed in the basement and in others it was installed in the front hall, where the residents could see it constantly... With no other change... electricity consumption was 30 percent lower in the houses where the meter was in the front hall.”***— Donella Meadows, Leverage Points: Places to Intervene in a System**

# Leverage Point 6: Information Flows

This is a powerful place. The structure of who knows what, who doesn’t, and when.

In systems thinking, **Information Flows** are about adding new feedback loops where they were missing. It’s not about strengthening existing loops, but creating entirely new ones. As Meadows showed, simply moving the electric meter—changing who had access to the information—was more powerful than any price change or energy-saving lecture.

This is one of the highest leverage points that is still cheap, fast, and easy to implement. If you do it right.

# The Trap

We have confused surveillance with sensitivity. We have mistaken logging for listening.

The modern enterprise is a data hoarder. We collect everything. Terabytes of logs, metrics, and traces, all pouring into a data lake that costs a fortune to maintain.

We call this “observability.”

It’s not.

It’s archaeology. It’s sifting through the ashes to figure out how the fire started. It’s performing an autopsy to determine the cause of death. The information flows in one direction: from the system into a database, where it sits until a human analyst—days or weeks later—has the time to ask a question.

The system itself learns nothing. It feels nothing. It is numb.

We are sold billions of dollars in shovels (SIEMs, log management, APM tools) to dig through the digital ruins. This is a market built on the premise that after-the-fact analysis is a security strategy. It isn’t. It’s an accounting strategy for assigning blame.

# The Shift

Stop performing autopsies. Start building a nervous system.

The body feels pain *now*. It doesn’t wait for a scheduled report. This sense of self-awareness, of knowing where your limbs are in space without looking, is called proprioception.

Our digital systems need it. They need to feel their own state in real time. The philosopher Michel Foucault understood the power of this information flow. Describing the Panopticon, a prison where a single guard can see every inmate, he noted that the *visibility* of power was its most effective tool.



“He who is subjected to a field of visibility, and who knows it, assumes responsibility for the constraints of power; he makes them play spontaneously upon himself; he inscribes in himself the power relation in which he simultaneously plays both roles; he becomes the principle of his own subjection.”— Michel Foucault, Discipline and Punish



This is the engineering principle behind the Observer dimension of the Context Tensor. It’s not just about logging. It’s about making the system aware that it is being watched. The presence and nature of an observer changes the physics.

An unobserved agent has more inherent risk. Its actions have more Mass. An agent operating in a fully instrumented Blue Zone, under the eye of a trusted monitor, is subject to a different set of physical constraints than one operating in the wild (i.e., the Legacy Internet).

The system doesn’t need to be told it’s in pain. It feels the pain as increased Heat and applies Gravity instantly.

The information loop is closed in real time: `action -> environment -> physics response`.

This is the difference between a system that is merely surveilled and a system that is truly self-aware. #leaveacomment

# The Landing

We have built a world of digital ghosts. Numb systems that generate mountains of data but have no idea what it feels like to be themselves.

We don’t need more data. We need more feeling. We don’t need better dashboards. We need proprioception.

Tomorrow, we reach the hinge. The point where everything changes. The place where we stop talking about suggestions and start talking about commands.
